How to get your security deposit back

Most deposit fights come down to one question: what did the place look like when you moved in? Whoever has proof usually wins.

At move-in

  • Walk every room before you unpack. Photograph walls, floors, carpets, appliances (inside too), windows, doors and fixtures.
  • Get close-ups of every existing scratch, stain or crack, with a date on each photo.
  • Write down the condition of each item: good, worn or damaged.
  • Send a copy to your landlord within the first few days and keep proof you sent it.

At move-out

  • Clean properly and fix the small things you caused, like nail holes.
  • Take the same photos from the same angles so the before and after line up.
  • Return every key and get a receipt, then give your forwarding address in writing.

If the landlord keeps money

Normal wear and tear (faded paint, light carpet wear, small scuffs) usually can't be charged to you. Ask for an itemized list of deductions in writing. Then reply with your evidence: the move-in condition, the move-out condition, and a clear request for the amount you're owed by a specific date. Many places set a deadline for landlords to return deposits, so check your local rules.
