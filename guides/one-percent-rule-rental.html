The 1% rule for rental properties

The 1% rule is a quick filter: a rental is worth a closer look if its monthly rent is at least 1% of the purchase price.

Example

A house costs $180,000. One percent is $1,800. If it rents for $1,800 a month or more, it passes the quick test. If it rents for $1,300, it probably won't cash flow well.

Why it isn't enough

  • It ignores property tax and insurance, which vary a lot from one city to the next.
  • It ignores repairs, empty months and management.
  • In many expensive areas almost nothing passes, while some cheap properties pass but cost a fortune in repairs.

What to check next

Monthly cash flow: rent minus every expense. This is the number that actually pays you.

Cap rate: yearly net income divided by the price, useful for comparing properties.

Cash-on-cash return: yearly cash flow divided by the cash you put in.
