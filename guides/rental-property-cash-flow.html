How to tell if a rental property will make money

A rental that looks cheap can still lose money every month. Run these numbers before you make an offer.

1. Start with realistic rent

Use what similar homes nearby actually rent for, not the listing's best guess. Then plan for empty months: setting aside 5 to 8% of the rent for vacancy is common.

2. Count every expense

  • Property tax and insurance
  • Repairs and maintenance (often 5 to 10% of rent)
  • Big-ticket replacements like the roof, water heater and HVAC (often 5 to 10% of rent)
  • Property management, even if you'll do it yourself at first (around 8 to 10%)
  • Utilities, HOA fees and anything else you pay

3. Work out the three key numbers

Cash flow is rent minus all expenses and any financing payment. Positive is good; it's what lands in your pocket.

Cap rate is yearly net income (before financing) divided by the price. It lets you compare properties fairly.

Cash-on-cash return is yearly cash flow divided by the cash you put in. It tells you how hard your own money is working.
